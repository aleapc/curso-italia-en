💡 Useful to know

  • ☕ Bars legally charge two prices for the same drink: al banco (standing at the counter) and al tavolo (at a table), and table service often costs about double — the price list must be posted, so check it before you sit.
  • 🧾 Coperto is a per-person cover charge: not required by law, but legal when clearly stated on the menu; servizio, printed as a percentage, is legal and owed.
  • 💶 Tipping is not expected in Italy — rounding up, or leaving €2–5 in cash after dinner for two, is appreciated and genuinely optional.
  • 🧑‍⚖️ The scontrino fiscale (fiscal receipt) is a legal requirement, not a courtesy — every transaction must come with one, so don't walk out without it.
  • 🚆 A regional digital ticket validates itself at the departure time you chose and can't be moved afterwards; keep the QR code openable on your phone, because not showing it counts as travelling without a valid ticket. Paper tickets still have to be stamped in the machine before boarding.
  • 💚 A green cross means pharmacy, for prescription and over-the-counter medicine. A closed one posts on its door the farmacia di turno list — the nearby pharmacies on the 24/7 rota, with phone numbers.
  • 🩺 An EHIC or GHIC card gives EU and UK visitors reduced-cost access to the public health system, but it covers neither private clinics nor repatriation.
