Permesso and scusi are not interchangeable

English says 'excuse me' for both. Italian splits them: permesso announces a movement you are about to make through space, scusi opens a question or repairs a bump. Using scusi to squeeze past works, but permesso is the word the situation wants.

Ciao is not your word yet

Ciao is informal and mutual — it belongs between people who already tutoiate each other. To a shopkeeper, a waiter or anyone behind a counter, buongiorno and arrivederci are the safe pair. You will hear ciao constantly; hearing it and using it are different permissions.

Buongiorno has a shelf life

It runs from morning to somewhere in mid-afternoon, when buonasera takes over. The changeover is fuzzy and regional, and nobody is offended by an early buonasera. Buonanotte is only for leaving at the end of the night, not for greeting.

The greeting is the transaction opening

Walking into a small Italian shop and going straight to the merchandise skips the move that says you are a person. It is not rudeness in your head, but it reads as rudeness in the room — and it costs nothing to fix.
